# Core Tenet 06 — Scenario Response Profiles

## Principle

Sports Hedge should answer:

> When this specific football situation occurs, how does this team historically respond — and how is that response different from the league?

This is the purpose of **Scenario Response Profiles (SRP)**.

## Core metric

The **Scenario Response Coefficient (SRC)** should summarize how strongly and reliably a team responds to a scenario relative to its own baseline and the league benchmark.

Raw correlation may be shown, but it must not be the headline metric.

## Non-negotiables

Every team/scenario/metric/window result should retain, where available:

- team baseline;
- team scenario value/effect;
- league baseline;
- league scenario value/effect;
- team excess response;
- sample size;
- confidence/uncertainty;
- stability;
- data quality;
- manager/regime context.

Scenarios must be structured/configurable, not hard-coded prose.

## Initial priority scenarios

Examples include:

- favourite concedes first;
- underdog takes the lead;
- favourite trailing after 60/70 minutes;
- team/opponent red card by score state;
- early goal scored/conceded;
- key starter omitted;
- defender/holding midfielder booked early;
- short-rest / post-European fixture context.

Initial metrics prioritize corners, yellow cards, red cards and goals.

## Time response

At minimum, support response windows such as 5m, 10m, 15m, 30m and remainder of match.

## Review checks

- Is the result team-specific rather than only league-wide?
- Is the league benchmark visible?
- Is N/confidence/stability visible?
- Are small samples shrunk/treated cautiously rather than overclaimed?
- Does any value claim then pass through the separate odds-weighted Research logic?
